## The five-field brief (STEP 0 input)

```
TOPIC: Stalinův pomník na Letné: proč ho komunisté postavili, tajně odpálili a proč ho Praha platí dodnes

ANGLE: Hidden-cost. Everyone knows the "fronta na maso" was blown up in 1962; the real story is the bill: Prague paid to build a statue that was obsolete before it was finished (a football stadium, 140 million Kčs, workers in prison-norm barracks, the sculptor's life), paid again to erase it without witnesses, and is still paying, because the 17,000-tonne monument could never be fully erased: its hollow plinth leaks and cracks, the city patches it every year, a councillor wants to pour foam concrete into it, and Praguers still call the place "Stalin".

PROOF BANK:
→ October 1962: before blasting, workers broke the statues' heads off with jackhammers; the fear was that Stalin's head would roll down the slope onto the bridge; the party's instruction was no film, no photos, no media mentions [S8; heads removed first and photography ban also in S1]
→ The monument: 15.5 m tall, 22 m long, 12 m wide, reinforced-concrete core clad with 235 granite blocks from Liberec-area quarries, 17,000 tonnes, cost 140 million Kčs, largest group statue in Europe [S1, S4 (official plaque text), S5, S17]
→ The slope could not carry it, so a two-storey basement on reinforced-concrete pillars was built under it; architecture historian Zdeněk Lukeš: the underground was originally meant as a monumental hall or a communist mausoleum, and in his view excavators pushed the demolition rubble down into it [S8, S7]
→ 2021: archaeologists in Letenské sady excavated the wooden barracks of the builders; lead archaeologist Jan Hasil: "vězeňská norma 3,5 metru čtverečního na osobu" [S9, S10]
→ Otakar Švec: pre-war star sculptor (Masaryk portraits in the 1920s, futurist Motocyklista held by the National Gallery); denunciations and StB checks on his studio; wife Vlasta took her life in 1954; he took his own life weeks before the 1 May 1955 unveiling; even his death date is disputed (4 April vs 3 March 1955) [S2, S1, S8]
→ Demolition: decision 1961; wooden enclosure in autumn 1962; three blasts, 19 Oct to 6 Nov 1962; heard across Prague; demolition cost 4.5 million Kčs; the statue stood 7.5 years; sculptor Josef Klimeš, who had worked on the build as a student, dressed as a worker and photographed it [S1, S5, S8]
→ Afterlife: underground used as storage until 1989, then rock club and first private radio in Czechoslovakia; 1991 metronome (25 m, 7 t) had to be flown in by helicopter because nobody trusted the blasted plinth with a crane [S5, S3]
→ Still paying: 2019 underground closed for collapse risk; 2026 city council fight over the "Stalin" cultural centre, statics reports of leaking and damaged load-bearing elements, annual patching, councillor Adam Zábranský's foam-concrete proposal; locals call the place "Stalin" [S1, S14, S15, S16, S13]

HOOK DIRECTION: Lead with October 1962: workers on Letná breaking the head off a 15.5 m Stalin with jackhammers so it would not roll down onto the bridge, under a no-photos order, on the exact spot where viewers today arrange to "meet at Stalin". Tension planted: the same Communist Party that spent 140 million on it was now erasing it in secret; why, who paid for it, and why is Prague still paying today?

STRUCTURE HINT: Documentary with a true-crime cold open (most dramatic moment first, then rewind), compressed to five short chapters for a 5-minute explainer: the order, the build (cellar planted as the payoff thread), the sculptor, the erasure, the bill today. The BUT/THEREFORE chain is already in the events (slope too weak → cellar → cellar is the 2026 problem).
```

## Angle-mining pass (how the angle won)

**Default sentence:** "Komunisté postavili na Letné obří sochu Stalina a po destalinizaci ji v roce 1962 vyhodili do vzduchu."

- **Contrarian:** the statue was obsolete before it was finished (Stalin died 1953, unveiled 1955, condemned 1956), and it was the same party, not the public, that blew it up. Strong, but on its own it only extends the default sentence slightly.
- **Untold-story:** Otakar Švec, the sculptor who never saw it unveiled. Well proven [S2], but already the subject of the ČT film *Monstrum*, the book *Žulový Stalin*, Szczygieł's *Gottland* and a 2026 university podcast (see scan below). Used as one chapter, not the spine.
- **Mechanism-reveal:** how you erase 17,000 tonnes secretly (heads first, wooden screen, three blasts). Visual and proven [S1, S8], but "Jak se to stavělo" (36 min) already covers build-to-blast.
- **Hidden-cost (winner):** the bill, then and now. It contradicts the default sentence ("1962 was the end") with documented 2019-2026 evidence that the city is still paying for the plinth, and it lets the other three frames serve as chapters. Proof is the strongest of the four because it is current and specific (council decisions, statics reports, named councillors).

## Cut for lack of verification (verify-or-cut)

- **800 kg of explosives.** Only English Wikipedia states it, citing a 2005 Radio Prague piece I could not open (JS wall, Wayback blocked). Another search result claimed "two tons of explosives, two thousand detonators". The sources conflict, so it's cut. The script says "three blasts" instead [S5].
- **"World's largest Stalin statue."** This comes from English Wikipedia and an unopened Radio Prague headline. The Czech sources consistently say "largest group statue in Europe", so the script uses that.
- **31,733 granite blocks** [S8] conflicts with **235 blocks** (Rudé právo 1955, the official plaque, ČTK). The script uses 235.
- **Stalin's left leg weighed 40 t** (only totalita.cz), plus **boots 2 m and coat button 60 cm** ([S8], phrased as planned dimensions, "měly měřit"). Cut: single or ambiguous sources.
- **Who tapped the foundation stone, and the exact date.** Gottwald on 21 Dec per [S1], Gottwald on 22 Dec per [S11], Zápotocký on 22 Dec per [S5]/[S13]. The sources conflict, so it's not used.
- **Forced labour / PTP built it.** Only a hypothesis: ČT24 quotes historian Petr Blažek doubting it, and the Guardian says "believed". The script states only the verified barracks plus Hasil's prison-norm quote.
- **"Švec didn't want to win / didn't want to make it."** The only source is [S11] "podle některých badatelů". The audit cut it as a weasel attribution (H14).
- **"Kam se poděla žula... se dodnes neví."** I only saw this as a Czech Radio headline and did not open the article. The script uses the opened Lukeš quote [S7] instead.
- **"Radio Stalin" as the pirate station's name** (English Wikipedia only). The script says "first private radio in Czechoslovakia" [S5].
- Cut for length, not verification (all verified): the Michael Jackson statue in 1996 [S5], the ODS billboard in 1998 [S5], the 1953 currency-reform strike on the site with Zápotocký visiting [S8], and the "Náš úkol musí být splněn do 28. 10. 1954" scaffold banner [S13].
