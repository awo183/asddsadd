# Uranium for Stalin

**A ~10-minute English documentary** on the question:
*What role did Czechoslovak uranium play in the first Soviet atomic bomb, and what were the brutal conditions in the uranium mines and camps where the communist regime used political prisoners to mine uranium for the Soviet Union?*

- `Uranium_for_Stalin_web.mp4` — the film (1080p, H.264/AAC, English subtitle track; small enough for GitHub).
- `subtitles.en.srt` — the same subtitles as a separate file.
- [`SCRIPT.md`](SCRIPT.md) — full narration with fact notes and sources.
- [`CREDITS.md`](CREDITS.md) — every archive clip and photo with author and licence.

Real footage and photographs: Wikimedia Commons archive material (U.S. National Archives and Air Force films, a 1937 Soviet newsreel, 1920s–40s photos of Jáchymov, 1950s aerial survey photographs of the camps, present-day photos of the camp sites, the Red Tower of Death and the Vojna memorial). Footage of generic 1940s mining is labelled *illustrative* on screen. Animations: animated maps (Europe → Ore Mountains, Jáchymov → Semipalatinsk, the route east, the camp regions), a uranium-deliveries chart, the uranium → plutonium → bomb chain, the radon decay chain, a reconstruction of the 1945 agreement (labelled as an illustration), a timeline, a prisoner count, and a cross-section of the Tower of Death.

## Rebuilding / rendering on a GPU

Everything is generated by code in [`pipeline/`](pipeline) — no paid APIs or keys are needed:

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt     # plus ffmpeg on PATH
./build.sh                          # downloads assets, voices, renders, muxes -> build/
ENCODER=nvenc ./build.sh            # require NVIDIA GPU encoding (h264_nvenc)
```

`render.py --encoder auto` (the default) uses **NVENC on an NVIDIA GPU when one is present** and otherwise falls back to libx264 on the CPU. The cloud container this was produced in has no GPU, so the committed film was encoded with x264; on a machine with an NVIDIA card the same command encodes on the GPU.

| Step | File | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| assets | `pipeline/fetch_assets.py` | downloads footage/photos from Wikimedia Commons (honours rate limits) and records licences |
| voice | `pipeline/tts.py` | English narration with Kokoro (local neural TTS), Czech names given as IPA |
| edit | `pipeline/timeline.py` | the edit decision list: which shot plays under which line |
| sound | `pipeline/audio.py` | original synthesised score + SFX, ducking under the voice, EBU R128 loudness |
| picture | `pipeline/render.py`, `anims.py`, `common.py` | Ken Burns stills, archive film, motion graphics, captions, grain → H.264 |
| subtitles / mux | `pipeline/subtitles.py`, `pipeline/mux.py` | SRT + final MP4 (and the <100 MB web copy) |

## Accuracy notes

Figures for prisoner numbers and total uranium output are historians' estimates and are presented as such. The exact amount of Jáchymov uranium inside the first Soviet bomb is not known, and the film says so: Czechoslovakia was one of the earliest suppliers of the Soviet programme alongside captured German stocks, Saxony, Bulgaria and Poland. Most political prisoners arrived in the uranium camps from 1949 onward, so their labour fed the Soviet arsenal that followed the first test; the film makes that distinction. See [`SCRIPT.md`](SCRIPT.md) for sources.
